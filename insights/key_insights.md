# Key Insights — Stock Market Performance & Risk Analysis

## Performance Insights

**Tesla achieved the highest total return (~20,300%) among all ten companies**, but this figure spans a longer holding period than several peers, so it should be read alongside its risk profile rather than in isolation.

**Amazon and Netflix delivered strong returns (996% and 1,124% respectively)** with moderate volatility, positioning them among the more balanced performers in the group.

**Walmart and Uber posted the lowest total returns (94% and 2%)**, reflecting their more defensive or newer market positioning compared to the high-growth tech names in the dataset.

## Risk Insights

**Zoom and Uber recorded the highest annualized volatility (68% and 59%)**, consistent with their status as newer, more speculative stocks during the analysis period.

**Walmart showed the lowest volatility (21%)**, reinforcing its profile as a defensive, lower-risk holding relative to the other nine companies.

**Every company in the dataset experienced a maximum drawdown exceeding 28%**, with Zoom and Uber both surpassing -68% — meaning an investor entering at the peak would have seen more than two-thirds of their position's value erased before recovery.

## Comparative Insights

**Higher returns were generally, but not always, associated with higher volatility.** Tesla combined the highest return with high volatility (55%), supporting a risk-reward relationship — but Amazon achieved a strong 997% return with comparatively lower volatility (30%), suggesting more efficient risk-adjusted performance.

**The Stock Performance Score (a weighted blend of return, Sharpe ratio, volatility, and drawdown) ranks Tesla first (78.3/100) and Amazon second (46/100)**, indicating that while Tesla's raw return is highest, its risk-adjusted edge over Amazon is narrower than the return figures alone suggest.

**Apple and Microsoft, despite lower absolute returns, posted Sharpe ratios above 1.1**, among the highest in the group — signaling efficient risk-adjusted performance even without leading in raw returns.

## Volume Insights

**Trading volume patterns did not consistently track price performance**, indicating that investor attention (as measured by volume) does not always align with realized returns in this dataset.

## Trend Insights

**The companies in this dataset span different market entry points** (e.g., Zoom and Uber have shorter trading histories than Apple or Microsoft), which limits direct comparability of total return figures across the full ten-company set. Annualized/risk-adjusted metrics (Sharpe ratio, volatility) provide a more standardized basis for comparison than total return alone.

## Limitations

- Total Return % is calculated over each company's available trading history, which varies in length across the dataset — this affects direct comparability between longer-established firms (Apple, Microsoft) and recently listed ones (Uber, Zoom).
- The Sharpe Ratio assumes a risk-free rate of 0%, a simplifying assumption made due to the absence of a risk-free benchmark in the dataset.
- This analysis is based on historical price data only; it does not incorporate fundamental financial metrics (earnings, revenue, balance sheet data).
